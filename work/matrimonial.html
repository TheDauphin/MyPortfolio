Matrimonial

The masjid's marriage service, out of the filing cabinet and into one honest record.

Overview

Most masjids run their marriage service on paper. Forms in a binder, a volunteer's memory, and a WhatsApp thread between two imams. It works until the person holding it all steps back — then the institutional knowledge walks out the door with them.

We built software that gives a masjid one structured record per candidate, a private and auditable way to work through introductions, and a compatibility layer that turns gut instinct into something reviewable. I designed the information architecture, the interface, and the system it's assembled from.

The Problem

This is the most sensitive kind of data a community organisation holds, handled by people who are not administrators by trade.

  • 01Scattered records. The same candidate exists as a paper form, a spreadsheet row and someone's notes — none of them agreeing.
  • 02Privacy is non-negotiable. Who can see what, and when, had to be visible in the interface itself — not buried in a settings page.
  • 03Matching is subjective. Families weigh things differently. A single opaque "match score" would have been both useless and offensive.
  • 04Non-technical staff. If it needs training, it doesn't get used.
Approach

One record, one truth

Every candidate gets a single canonical profile — background, family, education, practice, preferences, and the notes a coordinator adds over time. Everything else in the product reads from it. No duplicate entry, no conflicting versions.

Compatibility you can argue with

Rather than a black-box score, the compatibility layer breaks into named dimensions and shows its reasoning — where two candidates align, where they diverge, and which preferences either side marked as non-negotiable. The software makes the case; the humans still make the decision.

Privacy made visible

Field-level visibility is expressed in the UI as a persistent state, not a hidden setting. A coordinator can always see, at a glance, what a candidate has chosen to share and with whom.

Built for the volunteer

Dense but calm: strong hierarchy, mono-spaced metadata, generous line length, and destructive actions kept deliberately far from routine ones.

Outcome

The product replaces a paper process that had no backup and no continuity. What I'm proudest of is the restraint: in a category full of dating-app patterns, this reads like an institution's record system — because that's what a masjid actually needs it to be.
