Overview

Muslim community events are everywhere and nowhere. A lecture gets announced in one WhatsApp group, a fundraiser on a masjid's Instagram story, a youth night on a flyer taped to a door. If you weren't already in the right group chat, you simply never heard about it.

Ummah Connect gives those events a single home — a place where organisers publish once and attendees discover by location, category and date. I led the design end to end: the name's visual identity, the product architecture, the interface, and the design system the engineering team builds from.

The Problem

Two audiences with opposite needs, and a platform that had to serve both without feeling like a compromise.

  • 01Organisers are volunteers, not marketers. Posting an event had to take under a minute on a phone, in between other responsibilities.
  • 02Attendees want relevance, fast. Location, time, whether it's family-friendly, whether it costs anything — answered before they tap anything.
  • 03Trust is the whole game. A community platform that looks improvised doesn't get used twice.
Approach

One event object, many views

I started at the data, not the screen. Everything on the platform is a single well-defined event record — time, place, host, category, audience, cost. Once that model was right, the feed, the map, the detail page and the organiser form all became different windows onto the same object instead of four screens fighting each other.

A minute to post

The organiser flow was cut to the smallest set of required fields, with everything optional pushed behind a second step. Drafts save automatically. A poster upload can stand in for a description.

Legible before decorative

The interface is quiet on purpose — strong typographic hierarchy, generous spacing, high contrast — so the content carries the personality. Ornament lives in the brand layer, not on top of the information.

Outcome

Ummah Connect is live and in the hands of real organisers. The design system has held up through every feature added since launch — new surfaces get built from existing components rather than redrawn, which is the only honest test of whether a system works.
